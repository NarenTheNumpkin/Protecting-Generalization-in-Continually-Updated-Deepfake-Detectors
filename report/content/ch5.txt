@chapter RESULTS AND DISCUSSION {ch:results}

This chapter reports the results in the order in which the questions arise. Section {sec:repro} checks that the base detector is reproduced and measures what an ordinary update costs. Section {sec:devresults} reports the development comparison that selected the studied update, and Section {sec:main} the main comparison on sealed data. Section {sec:generality} tests how general the result is, Section {sec:mechanism} analyzes the mechanism, and Section {sec:robustcal} covers robustness, calibration and computational cost. Section {sec:discussion} discusses what the results mean and where they stop. Unless stated otherwise, AUROC values are percentages and differences are given in AUROC points.

@section BASE DETECTOR AND THE COST OF UPDATING {sec:repro}

@subsection REPRODUCTION OF GenD {sec:reproduction}

Table {tab:repro} compares the scores reported for GenD with those obtained with our pipeline. With the released weights, the pipeline reproduces the reported Celeb-DF and DFD results within the bootstrap intervals. The model that we retrained with checkpoint selection on FaceForensics++ validation data only is equally strong on its own test set (98.21), but it is 3.1 points lower on Celeb-DF than the released weights, and 2.6 points below the reported value, and 4.3 points lower on DFDC-Preview. Part of the published cross-dataset performance therefore appears to depend on checkpoint selection with data from the target family. The accuracy reported by the official evaluation code uses the equal-error threshold computed on the test labels, which is not available in practice, so this project reports accuracy at a fixed threshold throughout.

@subsection THE COST OF AN UPDATE {sec:cost}

Starting from the released checkpoint, sequential fine-tuning on stream S1 learns every task, with a mean gain of 2.10 AUROC points, and forgets little, with an average forgetting of 0.79 points. By the usual continual learning metrics the update is a success. Two other costs, however, are large and consistent across seeds. Detection of the FaceShifter manipulation, which belongs to FaceForensics++ but is never trained, falls from 84.85 to 77.06 AUROC (range over three seeds 76.44 to 77.39). The accuracy of the base task at the fixed threshold 0.5 drops from 95.0% to 79.9%, although its AUROC remains above 95. Updating a generalizable detector therefore mainly damages two things that the standard metrics do not show: its generalization and its calibration. This finding motivates everything that follows.

@section DEVELOPMENT RESULTS {sec:devresults}

Table {tab:dev} and {fig:dev} report the development comparison, run with the released checkpoint on the development splits with three seeds. Restricting feature distillation to genuine faces improves the FaceShifter probe over symmetric distillation in all six paired comparisons, by 2.0 points on average, with higher AA and lower forgetting. Adding a prototype memory to GA changed nothing and was removed. GA and LwF were complementary: their combination reached 82.85 on the probe, about two points above either component alone, at a cost in plasticity, with a gain of 1.05 against 2.10 for sequential fine-tuning. On the basis of these results GA+LwF was selected, by the registered rule, as the configuration to confirm on sealed data.

The updates remain useful. Relative to the never-updated model, GA+LwF raises DFDC-Preview from 85.71 to 88.23, Celeb-DF from 91.92 to 94.41 and DFD from 97.38 to 98.15. It slightly improves the base task while keeping its fixed-threshold accuracy at 93.7%, compared with 84.7% after sequential fine-tuning. {fig:tradeoff} places every method in the plane of learning and generalization. Head-only fine-tuning attains the highest $U$ (88.11) only because it barely learns: its gain is 0.02 points and its AA essentially equals that of the never-updated model (93.32 against 93.27). GA and GA+LwF lie on the upper frontier among the methods that learn the new datasets. GA favors the trained datasets, with the highest AA (95.23), and GA+LwF favors the unseen ones. Neither exemplar replay nor joint training on the union of the few-shot sets is an upper bound for retention: both store data, yet both reach a lower $U$ than GA+LwF.

@fig figs/fig_tradeoff_all.png | 4.9 | fig:tradeoff | Learning versus generalization for all methods on stream S1

On the trained datasets no statistically significant difference between GA+LwF and its main competitors was detected, which does not establish that they are equivalent. With 32-frame predictions and intervals that resample identity clusters, the AA of GA+LwF does not differ significantly from that of sequential fine-tuning ($-0.22$, 95% interval $[-0.83,+0.42]$), EWC, replay, LwF ($-0.12$, $[-0.43,+0.17]$), symmetric KD ($-0.19$, $[-0.57,+0.21]$) or GPL ($-0.24$, $[-0.98,+0.52]$). It is higher than that of the never-updated model ($+1.52$, $[+1.07,+2.01]$) and of symmetric KD with LwF ($+0.14$, $[+0.01,+0.27]$), and lower than that of GA alone ($-0.59$, $[-0.90,-0.29]$).

{fig:unseen} shows where generalization is lost. On the real-world WildDeepfake videos every update slightly improves detection, from 90.91 to between 91.27 and 92.42, probably because the stream adds diverse face-swap examples. The loss is concentrated on DeepFakeFace, and above all on the fully synthetic text-to-image faces, where sequential fine-tuning falls from 78.02 to 65.16 while GA+LwF retains 73.77. Updates on face-swap data thus erode the sensitivity of the detector to generation families that are absent from the stream, and anchoring the genuine representation limits this erosion more than any other method.

@subsection RETENTION BY FORGERY FAMILY {sec:retention}

The unseen score averages forgery families that behave differently, and a difference that is not significant does not by itself show that performance is retained. Table {tab:retention} therefore reports the change relative to the never-updated detector for each family over the five training seeds, with 95% intervals from a two-level bootstrap over seeds and test units. For GA+LwF the aggregate change is $-0.19$ points, with a 95% interval of $[-0.62,+0.30]$; seed 0 alone gives $-0.09$ ($[-0.53,+0.43]$). The interval excludes losses larger than 0.62 points but does not exclude a small loss. In an exploratory analysis with a margin of one AUROC point, chosen after the point estimates were known and therefore not a prospective confirmation, the lower bound lies above $-1$. For LwF, GPL and sequential fine-tuning the intervals lie entirely below zero.

@subsection MECHANISM CONTROLS {sec:controls}

Distilling only genuine faces constrains fewer samples than distilling all faces, so a weaker constraint, rather than the choice of the genuine class, could explain the advantage of GA over symmetric distillation. The controls rule this out. Random-subset KD distills exactly as many samples per batch as GA, but chosen regardless of label; it reaches $U=86.94$, indistinguishable from symmetric KD (86.95), and GA exceeds it by 0.51 points (hypothesis M1). Fake-only KD, the mirror image of GA, is the worst distillation variant ($U=86.47$; GA better by 0.98 points, M2), and its AA is also lower (94.32). Within the combined method, replacing genuine-only feature distillation by symmetric feature distillation lowers $U$ by 0.34 points (M3). The effect is therefore tied to the genuine class and not to the number of distilled samples.

@section GENERALITY OF THE RESULT {sec:generality}

@subsection TASK ORDERS AND ENCODERS {sec:orders}

{fig:replication} repeats the comparison for orders S2 and S3 and for the Perception Encoder (PE) variant of GenD. For PE the experiments start from the authors' released checkpoint, since no clean retrain was available; its selection pool did not include WildDeepfake or DeepFakeFace. In all three settings the ranking is the same: GA+LwF attains the highest $U$, followed by GA, and all twelve replication hypotheses hold (Table {tab:hyp}). The weakest result is GA+LwF against sequential fine-tuning in order S2, whose interval barely excludes zero ($+0.69$, $[+0.06,+1.35]$). In that order sequential fine-tuning itself loses only 0.68 points, because the last update is DFD rather than Celeb-DF.

@fig figs/fig_replication_2x2.png | 5.3 | fig:replication | Unseen-panel score after all updates in four settings

The encoder replication gives the strongest result. With PE, plain updating also damages the trained datasets, whose AA falls from 94.43 to 91.98 for sequential fine-tuning and to 92.78 for LwF. GA+LwF keeps AA at 94.56 and raises the unseen score above that of the never-updated detector (81.24 against 80.12). The benefit of anchoring the genuine class is therefore not a peculiarity of the CLIP encoder.

The two methods trade plasticity in opposite directions. GPL learns the new datasets more completely, with a mean gain of 2.51 against 0.74 on S1, and reaches the highest DFDC-Preview score of all exemplar-free methods (92.28). It forgets the base task, however (AF 0.69, FaceForensics++ AUROC 95.99 against 98.32), and loses fixed-threshold accuracy on it (87.0% against 93.7%). On S1 the two methods reach the same AA (94.78). Because GPL changes the input of the frozen detector, its watermark alters the representation of every image, including genuine faces and unseen manipulations, whereas GA constrains exactly the representation of genuine faces. The contrast is largest with the PE encoder, where GPL loses 6.5 AA points and its fixed-threshold FaceForensics++ accuracy drops to 67.5%. The one measure on which GPL is better is text-to-image faces with PE (67.16 against 61.75). GPL also costs about three times as much training time, 43 against 15 minutes per stream, since every step needs two gradients through the full encoder.

@section ANALYSIS OF THE MECHANISM {sec:mechanism}

@subsection WHERE ALONG THE STREAM GENERALIZATION IS LOST {sec:stagewise}

Evaluating the intermediate models on the unseen panel ({fig:stagewise}) shows that the loss is not spread evenly over the stream. The first two updates leave the unseen score unchanged or slightly higher for every method except sequential fine-tuning after DFDC-Preview. Most of the loss occurs at the final Celeb-DF update, where sequential fine-tuning drops from 87.87 to 86.17 and GA+LwF from 88.38 to 87.88. This agrees with the order replication: when Celeb-DF is learned before DFD (order S2), all methods lose less. The datasets that most damage generalization are thus specific, and the protection offered by GA matters most at those updates.

@subsection REPRESENTATION DRIFT {sec:drift}

For a fixed probe set of genuine faces, stream manipulations and unseen manipulations, the mean angular drift $1-\cos(z,z_{\mathrm{base}})$ of every final model with respect to the initial model was measured. Across 22 runs, genuine-feature drift predicts the change in $U$ closely ({fig:drift}, Spearman $\rho=-0.95$), but the drift of manipulated features predicts it equally well ($\rho$ between $-0.93$ and $-0.96$), and the partial correlation of genuine drift given manipulated-feature drift is only $-0.22$. Drift therefore shows that overall stability of the representation predicts retention; it does not by itself isolate a genuine-specific mechanism, which is what the matched controls of Section {sec:controls} address. GA does change the composition of the drift as intended: its ratio of genuine to stream-manipulation drift is 0.68, against 0.75 for symmetric, random-subset and fake-only KD (0.69 for GA+LwF against 0.79 for symmetric KD with LwF).

@fig figs/fig_drift.png | 4.8 | fig:drift | Genuine-feature drift versus the change in unseen-panel score for 22 runs

The constraint also transfers beyond the data it is applied to, as the analysis of Section {sec:theory} requires. GA, trained on genuine faces of the stream datasets only, reduces the drift of genuine faces from WildDeepfake (0.088 against 0.098 for symmetric and random-subset KD and 0.121 for fake-only KD) and from DeepFakeFace (0.060 against 0.064, 0.065 and 0.076), neither of which any model saw.

On the grid of last-layer patch tokens ({fig:driftmaps}), sequential fine-tuning changes every patch, most strongly at the border and in the background, and GA+LwF reduces this drift by a factor of four, almost uniformly. A 10-nearest-neighbor classifier separates genuine faces from unseen manipulations with almost the same accuracy for every model (78.3% to 79.7%; {fig:tsne}), so the information needed to detect unseen manipulations survives every update in the local structure of the representation. What updates damage is the alignment of the representation with the linear decision function, consistent with Proposition 2.

Its accuracy at the fixed threshold is nevertheless the highest among the updated models (87.6% averaged over the four test splits, against 83.3% for sequential fine-tuning and 85.0% for LwF), because the base task keeps its operating point.

Neither diagnostic is a deployable procedure for choosing a threshold under the exemplar-free constraint: the first pools genuine development videos of all earlier tasks, which an exemplar-free update does not retain, and the second uses labelled data of the evaluation domain itself. The ranking advantage therefore extends to the low false-positive region of the ROC curve, but no update rule tested in this project keeps a threshold chosen on historical data valid on new domains.

An obvious remedy is to recalibrate after each update with the only data available, those of the current task. A bias or a Platt scaling map \cite{platt1999probabilistic} fitted on 25 genuine and 25 manipulated Celeb-DF development videos reduces the test ECE of GA+LwF from 0.072 to 0.054 with Platt scaling, but the same map roughly doubles the ECE on the unseen panel for every model (for GA+LwF from 0.081 to 0.167), because the slope learned on the newest task, above 2 for every updated model, sharpens all scores (Appendix 1). Recalibration on the newest data alone therefore does not transfer, and calibration after an update needs either a calibration set that covers several sources or an update rule that preserves it.

@subsection COMPUTATIONAL COST {sec:compute}

GA and GA+LwF add one forward pass of the frozen previous model per batch. The three-update stream takes 14.9 minutes against 10.7 for sequential fine-tuning, the same as LwF and symmetric KD, and peak GPU memory rises from 5.42 to 5.50 GiB. Nothing is stored between updates and inference is unchanged. Replay, in contrast, must store raw face videos of real people, and GPL takes about three times as long as GA+LwF to train.

@section DISCUSSION {sec:discussion}

@subsection INTERPRETATION {sec:interpretation}

The results support a consistent picture. For a parameter-efficient foundation detector, the representation of manipulated faces must change to accommodate a new forgery, but the representation of genuine faces need not. Unseen manipulations are implicitly compared against that genuine representation. Aligning only genuine faces, as DFWF proposed for spoofed speech, preserves it at a smaller cost in plasticity than aligning all faces. The matched controls show that the benefit is tied to the genuine class rather than to a weaker constraint, the weight sweep shows that it holds at every strength, and the comparison at matched plasticity shows that neither a stronger LwF nor a gentler fine-tuning reaches the same retention, while on S1 with the CLIP encoder alignment alone with a development-selected weight does slightly better than the combination. Logit distillation stabilizes the output distribution and was complementary at the pre-set weights. That an objective designed for audio transfers to visual face forgery detection, and outperforms a recent specialized visual method, suggests that the asymmetry between one shared genuine class and many manipulated classes is a property of forgery detection rather than of a particular modality.

@subsection PRACTICAL GUIDANCE {sec:guidance}

Three observations translate into recommendations for anyone who maintains a deployed detector. First, retention of generalization should be measured at every update, with data that the update never touches, because forgetting metrics on the trained datasets barely register the damage. Second, genuine-sample alignment is a useful baseline for exemplar-free updates of foundation detectors that tune normalization parameters, but it is not a guarantee of retention: text-to-image faces still lose performance and decision thresholds do not transfer. Its weight should be chosen on development data according to how much plasticity the application needs; on S1 with the CLIP encoder, alignment alone with a weight chosen this way matched or exceeded the combination with LwF in retention, learning and AA, so the LwF term may be unnecessary, although this was not verified in the other settings. Third, calibration should be monitored after every update. Fitting a recalibration map on the newest data alone improved calibration on the trained datasets but degraded it on unseen data, so a calibration set should cover more than the latest source.

@subsection LIMITATIONS {sec:limitations}

Several limitations qualify these conclusions. The update objective is prior work \cite{ma2021dfwf}; this project adds evidence about it in a new modality and regime. Retention is uneven across forgery families: the aggregate score hides a loss on text-to-image faces that no method avoided. Five seeds were run only for the main comparison on S1, all from the same GenD model, so the variability of the base training is not covered, and the development-selected GA-only configuration was tested only on S1. GPL was compared through our re-implementation, with unreported settings fixed in advance. The sealed panel was re-used across extension stages, although no decision was based on it, and the method was refined on development data before confirmation. Manipulated FaceForensics++ and DFD videos were cropped with masks; with label-independent crops DFD AUROC is 4.5 to 6.6 points lower for every model, although the comparison between methods changes little. DeepFakeFace consists of single images, WildDeepfake and DFDC-Preview are re-aligned from distributed crops, the nine-update stream draws on three datasets, and all detectors are GenD variants. The robustness study uses synthetic degradations, and the margin analysis is post hoc. Finally, GA learns the new datasets less completely than sequential fine-tuning, and its calibration on them is worse.
